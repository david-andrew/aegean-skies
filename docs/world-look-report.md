# Aegean geography and shared terrain pipeline

Branch **`t3/world-look`**, based on main **`41f48d3`**. Implementation checkpoint **`5bd972d`**, followed by the final documentation commit. Nothing pushed or merged; the main checkout was not modified. Scope follows the user's clarification: procedural modeling against the reference and reuse of existing rendering rules, with aesthetic review/tuning left to the user.

## What changed

The original ten islands, their positions, source asset, erosion bake, canonical geometry, towns and groves are preserved. Around them, geography v4 now has nine authored mainland/large-island regions, explicit gulfs and peninsula fingers, a long western channel island, 33 bent range segments and 12 basin/river corridors. The model is Aegean-inspired, not a GIS reconstruction. Major sampled peaks are about 2,904 m and 2,824 m on the mainlands and 2,287 m on the southern island; peak envelopes reach 2,950/2,390 m respectively.

There are **660** accepted principal/seeded smaller islands in six structured groups. Major-radius bins: **475** below 300 m; **147** from 300 m–1 km; **15** from 1–2 km; **13** from 2–5 km; **10** at least 5 km. Principal drowned summits anchor the groups; a truncated Pareto proposal fills them with elongated islands and rocks. All groups receive islands, with open water between them and the original ±20 km reserve intact. Rejection for overlap changes the final distribution from the analytic proposal.

New small islands reuse the original coastline warp and cliff/hill/rock/cay profiles, extracted without changing their arithmetic. Mainland coasts blend those same cliff/hill profiles using signed-distance curvature: sheltered concave bays favour beach/hill profiles; exposed headlands favour cliffs. Several independent ranges, warped basin floors and coastal drainage corridors replace single straight ridge stripes. The coastal data/queries remain usable for future harbor-site searches; no towns or harbors were added.

New ground now supplies the original material's erosion-ridge channel, 5 m normal footprint and fbm/noise tint. It uses the same limestone, sand, scrub/olive/pine colors, lighting, near detail and aerial perspective. Existing limestone bands receive pixel-footprint filtering on new land to avoid unresolved distant aliasing. The original-island shader specializes the shared material with these extensions disabled, preserving its shading path and cost. Resolved strata remain the original material, including on exposed high ground; this is a tuning point for the user's review, not a new palette.

The original erosion filter generates a small directional lookup at bake time; runtime applies it in the authoritative height source, not as visual displacement. New groves use the existing meshes, ecology thresholds, scale/shade variation, wind and object/haze shader. A fixed 64-slot cache reuses GPU instance buffers, preparing at most one grove per update. Trees stand on the canonical surface and appear only on published near terrain.

The earlier black-sky image was the numeric streaming test's deliberate `hide_sky()` harness. Complete-game captures confirm the existing sky/haze path; no sky, cloud, flight or sea-material style was changed. Sea depth and whole-world chart inputs were adapted to the new source.

## Choices where the old pipeline had no country-scale equivalent

- **Erosion reuse:** a 98,304-byte basis generated by the same filter, with its original scale/strength, three resolved octaves, eight slope directions, 8 m sampling and a 512 m period. Runtime blends directions, shifts phase by seed and fades detail off flats/beaches. It uses a moderate reference slope and neutral ridge/valley fade. It approximates rather than reproduces the full five-octave locally evaluated legacy erosion. That original bake stays untouched.
- **High elevations:** vegetation fades from 900–1,800 m into the existing bare limestone material, with no snow or new alpine vegetation. The same fade controls new groves. These thresholds are explicit tuning inputs.
- **Trees over a continent:** the original 26 m candidate grid and 3 m ecology slope footprint are retained. New trees are limited to canonical near tiles within 700 m; distant forest representation remains the shared ground material. Original whole-island groves keep their existing draw range. No houses means no house-adjacent cypresses on new land.
- **Chart and depth:** whole-world chart pixels span roughly 780 m, so the chart samples the common macro/profile shape without subpixel erosion. Near-water depth retains exact source queries; wholly dry/deep source cells safely skip erosion work.

The authored versus seeded controls, field meanings, versioning and rebuild instructions are documented in `docs/world-geography.md` (also copied beside this report as `geography-controls.md`). Existing material colors and tree meshes remain in their original files.

### Cold streaming cost and rejected alternatives

Evaluating the original erosion filter per point was rejected after a laptop measurement: cold terrain preparation peaked at **293.47 ms**. The baked directional basis reduced that to 66.04 ms; exact analytical gradients for bilinear macro interiors reduced it further. Final eight-tile contact bursts peak at **46.77 ms**, total 1.106 s over 120 preparation updates, versus about 12.27 ms peak for the prior simpler source. Canonical contact coverage still arrives in 41 updates in that probe; the eight-station wide-FOV test takes 30–44 updates.

This is a genuine cold-arrival cost increase. The cache/upload caps remain unchanged and settled frames avoid this generation work, but cold teleports can stutter. The CPU preparation probe includes software atlas updates, not GPU drawing. A measured four-tile alternative peaks at **26.19 ms**, but delays canonical coverage from **41 to 81 updates** (total preparation 1.084 s over 120 updates). This is the cheapest tuning tradeoff if shorter individual stalls matter more than arrival latency; the implementation retains the eight-tile contact burst.
