# Procedural painted materials

`src/paint.jo` supplies the shared stepped light ramp, screen-space paper tooth,
and a repeatable 256×256 pigment sheet. The sheet is generated by code on first
use, uses local cell hashes (never the world RNG), and has mipmaps. RGB encodes
rounded overlapping canopy normals; alpha carries a broad pigment wash. It is
one small shared texture, not a texture per tree, building or terrain tile.
No reference images or generated image assets enter the game.

The sea keeps its physical swells, depth maps, clear coves, shoreline opacity,
wakes and shadows. Dense small wind-aligned marks and tiny sun glints supply
near-field motion cues. The larger, sparser painted replacements were reverted
after playtesting lost the sense of speed; colour adjustments must preserve
that fine detail. The decorative ripples do not drive physics.

Both terrain renderers use the same light and pigment. Limestone strata have
irregular broken value shelves, warm exposed rock and cooler blue-grey shadow;
all strata are filtered in the distance. Rounded dappled vegetation masses
continue beneath and beyond the individual grove meshes. Meadow motion advects
the broad pigment wash with the existing wind drift, avoiding fine hash mottling. Canonical heights,
coastlines, contact normals and terrain streaming remain unchanged.

Town and aircraft meshes share stepped warm sun/cool shade. Existing door and
window triangles carry recess coordinates; this changes neither their geometry
nor emissive-window alpha. Roof pigment varies across surfaces. Aircraft retain
their vertex liveries and receive a small sun-facing rim. Sky and cloud grain
use the same paper helper at final screen resolution.

Grove wind motion fades between 180 and 420 metres, where the displacement is
subpixel. Cells wholly beyond that range use a separate shader without gust
noise, sway trigonometry or fine pigment/recess work. Tree counts,
meshes and the nearby animation are retained. Pigment detail also fades before
it becomes unresolved; the broad colors and lighting remain on every tier.

Atmosphere uses the same time/weather palette across sky, sea and opaque
materials. `sky_air_color` honours its supplied palette (including rain and
night) and reaches the haze colour exactly at the curved horizon. Clear-day
teal belongs in `light_at`, not a hard-coded replacement inside the sky shader.
`weather_light` dims both direct and ambient light under a deck, keeps lit faces
brighter than shaded ones, and restores clear light above the cloud ceiling.
Rain darkens the frame while the sea retains a broad, cool reflected sheen.

Aerial perspective integrates the coastal height falloff plus a thin upper-air
component, preserving nearby colour and separating distant ridges even from an
aircraft. The first 60 m retain their pigment. The existing bounded cloud pass
carries slanted rain curtains scaled by the saved squall severity; no new cloud
population or textures are allocated. Its existing depth/transmittance-masked
light shafts can also operate in moist daylight, not only at a low sun. All
these changes are cosmetic; weather scheduling, flight, visibility used by AI,
world randomness and save formats remain unchanged.


Summer material refinements (`t3/look-3`): plaster boxes carry UV tag 182, mapped
to material 3 by both town vertex paths. This is an explicit wall tag, not a
heuristic that would recolour white aircraft or pale doors. Detailed houses,
far proxies, chapels and harbour warehouses use the same neutral ambient fill;
positions, normals, vertex colours and triangle counts remain unchanged. Opening
recesses keep their distinct material 2 tag.

`summer_cover` in `plants.jo` mixes straw grass, grey-green maquis and pine by
height, the geographic south-facing component of the normal, existing ridge
information and a stable pigment/baked-tint patch field. Cover never rotates
with the sun or consumes the world RNG. Intermediate rocky slopes carry warm
scree; broad exposed patches and steep cliffs retain limestone. This replaces
the narrow ridge-dependent vegetation cutoff that produced pale scar streaks.
The same material runs on the original islands and streamed mainland.

Sea body colour has a separate optical-depth ramp: open shelves reach the deep
blue palette at 12 m, sheltered coves retain a 24 m ramp. Physical bathymetry,
seabed transparency, shoreline opacity, waves, wakes and shelter data are
unchanged. The restored pre-painted sea microdetail is preserved byte-for-byte:
no change to mark/glint density, scale, advection, intensity or reflection.
Only the shallow/deep body-colour interpolation weight changes. Low fast
passes (`AEGEAN_FLYING="60 150"`) belong in every future sea visual check.
The shader adds no texture, sampler, varying or world record.
