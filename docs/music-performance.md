# Procedural music performance

`tools/music_pack.py` builds note events from the licensed sources. Its deterministic
interpretation pass is in `tools/music_performance.py`. It changes performance, never
pitch content or the source MIDI. All tracks share a monotone tempo map, so a cadence
or final ritard does not pull the orchestra apart. Runtime seeking reads the baked time
map implicitly through the resulting events; there is no mutable humanization RNG.

The pass uses the KTH/Director Musices **rule families** as design guidance; it is an
independent, deliberately smaller implementation, not a reproduction of their coefficients:

* Phrase arch: candidate phrase boundaries near four/eight-bar landmarks move toward
  melody rests or long notes. A melody summit determines the dynamic arch. Tempo moves
  forward through the phrase and broadens at its ending.
* Faster uphill and agogic emphasis: small shared tempo changes follow rising melody
  and the phrase summit. A final ritard is integrated into the same shared time map.
* Duration contrast and punctuation: short/long note values affect articulation;
  stepwise wind/string lines can overlap, repeated notes rearticulate, and phrase ends
  allow a small breath or bow-change gap.
* Melodic emphasis: the top melody note is voiced above accompaniment, with section
  balance. This is not full harmonic analysis: there is no claimed harmonic-charge rule.
* Metre and style: tango and march accents, light inegales for the rag/foxtrot arrangement,
  waltz downbeats, and restrained island-dance accents. Swing preserves quarter beats.

The source MIDI can already contain expressive timing and dynamics. The added deviations
are consequently modest. Automatic phrase detection is a heuristic and cannot substitute
for a musician's annotated score. `data/music/performance.json` records its decisions so
phrase boundaries and applied effects can be inspected or revised.

Primary sources:

- [KTH performance-rule introduction](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/performance_intro.html).
- [KTH Phrase Arch demonstration and explanation](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/Texts/phrasing.htm).
- [Anders Friberg, A Quantitative Rule System for Musical Performance](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/publications/thesisaf/sammfa2nd.htm).

No external player or synthesis library is needed by the game. Instrument oscillations,
noise and resonances are generated by the game's own code.

## Instrument models

`data/music/instruments.json` is an editable bank of scalar acoustic parameters. It has
seven registers and three effort levels for each of sixteen families. `tools/music_timbres.py`
packs it into a roughly 49 KiB raw-deflate bank; it does not read audio. The runtime interpolates
pitch and effort, and clamps outside the measured register. Drum keys select the nearest
model rather than interpolating a kick into a cymbal.

Wind, reed and bowed sources build analytic cycles from early/late harmonic magnitudes.
They have attack, release, spectral evolution, breath/bow texture, delayed vibrato and small
pitch settling. Strings use three independently moving oscillators. Accordion reeds beat
against each other. Choir is an optional instrument family, unused by current scores;
this does not enable announcer speech.

Piano, harp, plucked strings, bass and percussion use independently decaying modes, with
fast and slow energy components. String stiffness raises the upper modes; percussion has
non-integer mode ratios and decaying broadband excitation. A two-loss feedback string
prototype was discarded after it fit the upper-partial decay worse than the modal model.
The island pluck remains a generic bright steel-string model, not a measured bouzouki.

These are compact approximations: 32 partials cannot reproduce all body/noise detail;
section detuning cannot model individual bow strokes; automatic phrase rules do not
interpret harmony or substitute for orchestral musicians. Harmonic-distance measurements
on the fitting grid validate spectral changes, not perceived acoustic realism.

There are at most 64 voices. Storage is allocated once, with two 512-point analytic cycles
and 32 modal states per voice. Seeking reconstructs modal amplitudes/phase directly at the
requested age and resets the hall tail; it never replays a song from its beginning.
`tools/musiclab.jo` renders files without opening an audio device.

## Record and receiver

`src/radio_chain.jo` separates record wear from reception. Music pieces have a fixed record
mix (light for the island programme, heavier for dance records); Morse has none. Record
noise survives strong reception, while RF noise grows as reception falls. All controls
are smoothed sample by sample, so block size and frame partition do not change the waveform.

The record stage has colored surface hiss and many small contacts in overlapping density
packets, fractional-delay wow/flutter, high-frequency groove overload, a 220–3600 Hz
recording band and an 850 Hz horn resonance. There are no isolated large pops. A small
1.3 Hz density modulation follows 78 rpm without adding an audible recurring click.
Noise density and wow depth are model choices, adjustable after listening.

The receiver adds envelope-controlled gain, asymmetric audio-stage distortion, 150 Hz
high-pass and 4300 Hz low-pass filters, cabinet/midrange resonances and a speaker rolloff.
Weak signals have slower level fading and a variable blend toward a 1450 Hz treble rolloff.
The original receiver noise band, 300–3000 Hz, underlies dense micro-crackle at weak
reception, with reduced white hiss and a modest programme-level lift. Night raises the
noise level; isolated atmospheric crash envelopes have been removed. Off-tuning raises the bass cutoff,
reduces treble, increases distortion and introduces an adjacent-carrier whistle.

Band and texture choices were informed by long-term spectra and quiet passages of period
transfers. Such recordings combine programme, microphone, film/disc, transmitter and later
transfer processing: energy percentiles are **not** measurements of receiver cutoff. The
chain is a plausible controllable model, not an identified reconstruction of one set.
`tools/radiochainlab.jo` accepts scratch float PCM for isolated chain comparisons.


## Solo-call and instrument follow-up

The opening call has a dedicated trumpet model, leaving ensemble brass separate. Its
upper-partial balance changes with effort, a short positive pitch transient marks the
attack, and a level-dependent brightness stage softens the tone as support falls. The
performed note targets have three effort points; `MUS2` extends the event format with a
peak and its location. The Adagietto uses `MUS3` for independent section gains; other scores retain `MUS1`.
Seeking reconstructs either part of the effort contour directly.

`opening-performance.json` is a coarse annotation of the solo, not a general performance
inference algorithm. It corrects the pickup rhythm and tied-note transcription, and gives
the call its measured pacing, unequal triplet spacing and crescendo. No recorded waveform
or source-file identifier is part of this annotation. The explicit annotation complements
the generic phrase rules; it does not prove those rules can infer every performance.

Clarinet register targets now concentrate on the repertoire's throat/clarion transition
and frequently played pitches, with an analytic closed-bore pulse and short reed noise.
Piano and island-pluck decay fits balance relative error in the body/tail against the
strike, rather than allowing the loud initial frames to dominate. The island voice has
a sharper plectrum transient and shorter release. The island pluck stays unchanged in the
next orchestral pass. See `music-orchestra.md` for the annotated Adagietto, concert hall,
trumpet, piano correction and station levelling; `music-playback-budget.md` records the
bounded caching plan and its gates.
