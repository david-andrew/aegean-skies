# Procedural music performance

The status of every piece, its treatment and where it is heard: [music-status.md](music-status.md).

`tools/music_pack.py` builds note events from the licensed sources. Its deterministic
interpretation pass is in `tools/music_performance.py`. It changes performance, never
pitch content or the source MIDI. All tracks share a monotone tempo map, so a cadence
or final ritard does not pull the orchestra apart. Runtime seeking reads the baked time
map implicitly through the resulting events; there is no mutable humanization RNG.

The pass uses the KTH/Director Musices **rule families** as design guidance; it is an
independent, deliberately smaller implementation, not a reproduction of their coefficients:

* Phrase arch: candidate phrase boundaries near four/eight-bar landmarks move toward
  melody rests or long notes. A melody summit determines the dynamic arch. Tempo moves
  forward through the phrase and broadens at its ending.
* Faster uphill and agogic emphasis: small shared tempo changes follow rising melody
  and the phrase summit. A final ritard is integrated into the same shared time map.
* Duration contrast and punctuation: short/long note values affect articulation;
  stepwise wind/string lines can overlap, repeated notes rearticulate, and phrase ends
  allow a small breath or bow-change gap.
* Melodic emphasis: the top melody note is voiced above accompaniment, with section
  balance. This is not full harmonic analysis: there is no claimed harmonic-charge rule.
* Metre and style: tango and march accents, light inegales for the rag/foxtrot arrangement,
  waltz downbeats, and restrained island-dance accents. Swing preserves quarter beats.

The source MIDI can already contain expressive timing and dynamics. The added deviations
are consequently modest. Automatic phrase detection is a heuristic and cannot substitute
for a musician's annotated score. `data/music/performance.json` records its decisions so
phrase boundaries and applied effects can be inspected or revised.

Primary sources:

- [KTH performance-rule introduction](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/performance_intro.html).
- [KTH Phrase Arch demonstration and explanation](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/performance/Texts/phrasing.htm).
- [Anders Friberg, A Quantitative Rule System for Musical Performance](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/publications/thesisaf/sammfa2nd.htm).

No external player or synthesis library is needed by the game. Instrument oscillations,
noise and resonances are generated by the game's own code.

## Instrument models

`data/music/instruments.json` is an editable bank of scalar acoustic parameters. It has
seven registers and three effort levels for each of sixteen families. `tools/music_timbres.py`
packs it into a roughly 49 KiB raw-deflate bank; it does not read audio. The runtime interpolates
pitch and effort, and clamps outside the measured register. Drum keys select the nearest
model rather than interpolating a kick into a cymbal.

Wind, reed and bowed sources build analytic cycles from early/late harmonic magnitudes.
They have attack, release, spectral evolution, breath/bow texture, delayed vibrato and small
pitch settling. Strings use three independently moving oscillators. Accordion reeds beat
against each other. Choir is an optional instrument family, unused by current scores;
this does not enable announcer speech.

Piano, harp, plucked strings, bass and percussion use independently decaying modes, with
fast and slow energy components. String stiffness raises the upper modes; percussion has
non-integer mode ratios and decaying broadband excitation. A two-loss feedback string
prototype was discarded after it fit the upper-partial decay worse than the modal model.
The island pluck remains a generic bright steel-string model, not a measured bouzouki.

These are compact approximations: 32 partials cannot reproduce all body/noise detail;
section detuning cannot model individual bow strokes; automatic phrase rules do not
interpret harmony or substitute for orchestral musicians. Harmonic-distance measurements
on the fitting grid validate spectral changes, not perceived acoustic realism.

There are at most 64 voices. Storage is allocated once, with two 512-point analytic cycles
and 32 modal states per voice. Seeking reconstructs modal amplitudes/phase directly at the
requested age and resets the hall tail; it never replays a song from its beginning.
`tools/musiclab.jo` renders files without opening an audio device.

## Record and receiver

`src/radio_chain.jo` separates record wear from reception. Music pieces have a fixed record
mix (light for the island programme, heavier for dance records); Morse has none. Record
noise survives strong reception, while RF noise grows as reception falls. All controls
are smoothed sample by sample, so block size and frame partition do not change the waveform.

The record stage has colored surface hiss and many small contacts in overlapping density
packets, fractional-delay wow/flutter, high-frequency groove overload, a 220–3600 Hz
recording band and an 850 Hz horn resonance. There are no isolated large pops. A small
1.3 Hz density modulation follows 78 rpm without adding an audible recurring click.
Noise density and wow depth are model choices, adjustable after listening.

The receiver adds envelope-controlled gain, asymmetric audio-stage distortion, 150 Hz
high-pass and 4300 Hz low-pass filters, cabinet/midrange resonances and a speaker rolloff.
Weak signals have slower level fading and a variable blend toward a 1450 Hz treble rolloff.
The original receiver noise band, 300–3000 Hz, underlies dense micro-crackle at weak
reception, with reduced white hiss and a modest programme-level lift. Night raises the
noise level; isolated atmospheric crash envelopes have been removed. Off-tuning raises the bass cutoff,
reduces treble, increases distortion and introduces an adjacent-carrier whistle.

Band and texture choices were informed by long-term spectra and quiet passages of period
transfers. Such recordings combine programme, microphone, film/disc, transmitter and later
transfer processing: energy percentiles are **not** measurements of receiver cutoff. The
chain is a plausible controllable model, not an identified reconstruction of one set.
`tools/radiochainlab.jo` accepts scratch float PCM for isolated chain comparisons.


## Solo-call and instrument follow-up

The opening call has a dedicated trumpet model, leaving ensemble brass separate. Its
upper-partial balance changes with effort, a short positive pitch transient marks the
attack, and a level-dependent brightness stage softens the tone as support falls. The
performed note targets have three effort points; `MUS2` extends the event format with a
peak and its location. The Adagietto uses `MUS3` for independent section gains; other scores retain `MUS1`.
Seeking reconstructs either part of the effort contour directly.

`opening-performance.json` is a coarse annotation of the solo, not a general performance
inference algorithm. It corrects the pickup rhythm and tied-note transcription, and gives
the call its measured pacing, unequal triplet spacing and crescendo. No recorded waveform
or source-file identifier is part of this annotation. The explicit annotation complements
the generic phrase rules; it does not prove those rules can infer every performance.

Clarinet register targets now concentrate on the repertoire's throat/clarion transition
and frequently played pitches, with an analytic closed-bore pulse and short reed noise.
Piano and island-pluck decay fits balance relative error in the body/tail against the
strike, rather than allowing the loud initial frames to dominate. The island voice has
a sharper plectrum transient and shorter release. The island pluck stays unchanged in the
next orchestral pass. See `music-orchestra.md` for the annotated Adagietto, concert hall,
trumpet, piano correction and station levelling; `music-playback-budget.md` records the
bounded caching plan and its gates.

## Clair de lune, shaped by a pianist's annotation

The Mutopia transcription of Clair de lune plays every note at one velocity, in strict time.
`data/music/clair-de-lune-performance.json` is its annotation, written by
`tools/clair_performance.py` from a readable plan, bar by bar (72 bars of 9/8). The generic phrase
rules are switched off for this piece (its `CONFIG` timing, dynamics and ritard are zero) so the
annotation alone shapes it, through the same `apply_performance_map` as the Adagietto, which gains
four optional layers (absent for the Adagietto, whose output is unchanged):

* `time_map` on eighth notes: a phrase-level tempo arch with rubato, lingering into each phrase's
  cadence, pushing through the Un poco mosso and En animant, broadening at the climax, Calmato,
  Tempo I and a long morendo (about 346 s performed from 328 s of score);
* `dynamics` in dB (pp opening, the swell through the arpeggios to the ff of En animant, a hushed
  return, fading out), part of it as touch (`effort_share`, which darkens or brightens the piano's
  tone) and the rest as level (`MUS3` mix gains);
* `voicing`: the right hand's top note of each chord brought out over the inner notes and the
  left hand;
* `pedal`: sustain spans changed with each beat in the chordal sections and each bar through the
  arpeggios (lifted 60 ms at each change), holding notes up to six seconds; `spread_seconds`: each
  chord rolled 30 ms from the bass up.

Rebuild: `python tools/clair_performance.py && python tools/music_pack.py`.

## Expressive timing from the score (tools/expressive_timing.py)

A general system, not hand-drawn bars. For any piece it measures an intensity curve from the
notes, on half beats: density (notes per beat), motion (distinct onsets per beat: runs and
arpeggios), register and range, tension (how dissonant the sounding pitch classes are), dynamics
(the score's markings when given, else velocities that vary), and the melody's rise; each is
normalised against the piece itself, weighted and smoothed over about a bar. The tempo then:

* moves on where the texture flows (motion, scaled by how loud the music is, so a quiet flowing
  passage doesn't rush) and where intensity builds;
* broadens at intensity peaks and lingers into phrase ends (phrases every few bars, moved to a
  nearby rest or long note in the melody), with a final ritard;
* takes the pace of each section from the score's printed tempo markings when given (Un poco
  mosso, Calmato, Tempo I...), the intensity shaping the motion within them;
* wanders a little by a seeded, bounded variation (slow waves and a small offset per phrase), so
  it isn't mechanical yet every rebuild is identical;
* is smoothed over a beat and eased into its range with a soft limit (no flat ceilings).

Parameters per piece (`ExpressiveTiming`): base pace, range, sensitivity, the weights of flow,
build, peaks and cadences, the variation and its seed, the phrase length, the tempo markings.
Clair de lune uses it through its annotation (`tools/clair_performance.py`, with its dynamics plan
as the markings); the Berceuse through `music_pack.EXPRESSIVE`. The Gymnopédie keeps its steady
reading (the system pushes on with each entry of the tune, against its "Lent et douloureux"),
and the Adagietto keeps its measured clock (the computed curve correlates only weakly with it,
0.10).

### Arpeggios as one gesture across the hands

`arpeggio_gestures` follows each arpeggio line through the notes that are alone in their hand
(a hand playing two notes at once is a chord or a melody in thirds), joining the line that has
just sounded close in time and pitch, whichever hand plays it. Each sweep up and back gets one
touch and one smooth contour rising to its highest note, so a figure passing from the left hand
to the right is one continuous line in loudness. Clair de lune's voicing then brings the melody
only a couple of dB over the accompaniment, the left hand lifted: hand-crossing steps fell from
a mean of 9.2 dB to 1.6 dB, the right hand's average over the left from 7.3 to 3.2 dB.

## Moods

`MOODS` in `tools/expressive_timing.py` are presets over the same intensity analysis: mournful,
tender, dreamy, stately, dance-like, jaunty, triumphant and agitated. Each sets how intensity turns
into tempo (in a lament building intensity weighs the music down, `build` < 0; when agitated it
presses), the rubato's depth and where it lands (`lead_in` hesitates into each phrase, `cadence`
lingers at its end), and the touch (`Touch`): the phrase dynamics, legato or detached lines, the
piano's pedal and the melody's lean. Every piece's mood is classified by hand in
`tools/music_pack.py` (`MOOD`, with its reason, copied into `data/music/catalogue.json`);
`APPLY_MOOD` lists the pieces actually played in their mood. So far that is the Gymnopédie, in
both its scorings (mournful: a base of about 69 quarter bpm, 63–70 in the body, easing back as
each phrase of the tune swells and hesitating into each phrase, pedalled by the bar, legato). A
mood may cap its own easing: mournful's `max_slow` keeps every dip within 10% of the piece's base
tempo (the final ritard apart), so a lament still flows whatever piece is given the mood. (A first
reading slowed to 45–57 bpm and was too heavy.) The rest keep their current reading until their mood's result
is shown to be better.

## The pianos, and a gentler touch

There are two pianos:
- **The concert grand** (the original piano model). Clair de lune is played on it.
- **The 1920s hotel upright** (`MUSIC_UPRIGHT`, the 17th timbre model), a honky-tonk. It was
  fitted the same way to FluidR3 GM's Honky-tonk Piano (see `data/music/SOURCES.md`), keeping its
  natural loudness across the keyboard (its top quieter than the grand's equal-energy notes). It
  adds:
  - its unison: the first eight partials sound as two equal strings 5 cents apart, beating;
  - duller hammers below velocity 90 (the reference has one velocity layer, so softer-is-darker
    is the game's own rule);
  - a key knock and a damper thump, very low;
  - a quicker damper.

Which piano plays follows the venue, not the piece: the bar's band always plays the upright; the
radio's classical programme uses the grand and its popular and dance programmes the upright. See
`docs/music-orchestra.md`, "Which piano: the venue decides".

`GENTLE` sets a gentler touch per piece (`gentle_touch`): a soft mean velocity per voice (the tune,
the right hand's chords, the bass, the left hand's chords), keeping a third of the phrasing around
it. The Gymnopédie is played this way. Its orchestral version (`gymnopedie-orchestra`) sets the
tune about +1 LU over the accompaniment by its arrangement levels.

## Automatic balance

Two build-time passes balance the band and orchestra without a listener. Both render stems with the
game's own synthesizer (`tools/music_stems.jo`).

### Part balance (tools/music_part_balance.py)

Every part of every piece gets one gain, so the tune sits forward and what is behind it is heard.

1. **Parts.** The arranged, interpreted notes (`music_pack.build`) are split into parts: an
   instrument on its staff (`strings@3`; a keyboard's or a harp's staves are one part, the
   percussion one). A piece with one pitched part has nothing to balance.
2. **Measure.** Each part is rendered alone, whole, in the piece's hall and at its level
   (`MUSIC_EVENTS`, a scratch-packed score), and its loudness taken by ITU-R BS.1770: K-weighted,
   400 ms blocks, gated, so only the span where the part plays counts.
3. **Roles**, read from the notes in 4-second windows, since a part's role changes: the melody (the
   staff marked as the melody in `music_performance.CONFIG` wherever it plays moving notes: a
   soloist under the orchestra's top line, a singer's part; otherwise the part holding the top line
   among the notes that are new, so a held pedal over the tune does not hide it), a doubling (the
   melody's notes at the unison or the octave), the bass (the lowest line, low), a counter-melody
   (one line, moving by step, in its own rhythm), the harmony (the rest), percussion.
4. **Everything that plays is heard.** In each window an accompanying part is compared with the
   median of the parts accompanying with it (the other one, of two), after its role's place (bass and counter-melody level, the
   inner harmony 2 LU under them, a part in the melody's own register up to 1.5 LU further under,
   where it masks the tune). A part more than 8 LU under, on the median of its windows, is raised
   to that; a doubling more than 6 LU under the part it doubles is raised to that. Nothing is
   lowered here.
5. **The tune over the rest.** The melody's partial loudness (with its doublings) over everything
   pitched behind it: loudness summed over critical bands, each counted only above what the other
   masks there (N = (Em + Ea)^0.23 − Ea^0.23). This is the measure the pieces balanced by ear agree
   on: the Adagietto −2.2 and the orchestral Gymnopédie −0.6, where the plain loudness difference
   puts them 10 LU apart (−2.8 and +7.0). A piece between −2.5 and +4 is left. One above is
   brought to +3 by moving the tune's parts and the rest together; one below is brought to −1.5
   by moving them apart. A part that does both is moved by its share. Two limits: the parts behind the tune,
   together, are never raised nearer than 2 LU under the tune's own plain loudness (a dull bass
   carries more energy than it sounds, but only so far; the pieces the partial loudness does
   settle come out at +2 to +5), and a tune under its accompaniment is raised by at most 4 dB (a
   buried top line is as often not the tune).
6. **Apply and verify.** The gains are shifted together so the mix's gated loudness is unchanged;
   the balanced piece is rendered whole and trimmed until its BS.1770 loudness equals the
   unbalanced piece's on `tools/music_level.py`'s own meter (the 48 kHz file), so its level in
   `data/music/levels.json` stays valid. Percussion is measured
   and left where the score put it. No part moves more than 20 dB; a move past 8 dB is flagged
   `suspicious` for a listen.

`data/music/part-balance.json` holds each piece's status, gains, each part's role and loudness
before and after, the melody over the rest before and after (plain and partial), and the hash of
the notes it was measured on. `tools/music_pack.py` applies the gains as mix gains (a plain level:
the touch and its tone are untouched) and records them in `data/music/performance.json`
(`part_balance`). A piece whose notes have changed since is recorded there as `stale` and packed
without gains; `tests/music_part_balance_test.py` fails until it is balanced again.

Pieces balanced by hand (`music_pack.HAND_SET_BALANCE`: the Adagietto, the trumpet call, Clair de
lune, both Gymnopédies, and the title's music) are measured and reported, never changed; nor are
the four pieces that already had a balance in `performance.json` (`balance_db`, per instrument,
from `tools/music_balance.py`: the Magnetic Rag, El Choclo, Ave Maria, Adelita). `--calibrate` prints what they
measure and what the balance would have asked of them (under 2.5 dB for each part).

Rebuild: `python tools/music_part_balance.py [piece ...] --report table.md && python
tools/music_pack.py` (the whole catalogue: about an hour of CPU, cached by the notes' hash, so a
changed piece costs seconds). It needs numpy, scipy, mido and `sloppy`.

### Harshness shelves (tools/music_balance.py)

The earlier pass, on the first thirteen pieces. Harshness per instrument: the presence band
(2–5 kHz) against the body (0.3–2 kHz), Zwicker-weighted sharpness and presence-band flux; a part
past its instrument's allowance (set from the parts already approved by ear) gets a high shelf
above 2.2 kHz in the synth (`MusicPiece.shelf_db`, applied to its partials). It also set the melody
3 LU of partial loudness over the accompaniment, one gain per instrument, for four pieces: those
gains are kept (`balance_db`). Shelves and gains are in `data/music/balance.json`.

Rebuild: `MUSIC_BALANCE=0 python tools/music_pack.py && python tools/music_balance.py &&
python tools/music_pack.py` (about a minute and a half; `--measure` re-measures the packed result).

## Grace notes

`tools/music_grace.py` plays the sources' small notes as grace notes; `tools/music_pack.py` applies it
after the part balance (which is keyed on the notes before it), so a grace note keeps its part's
balance and no piece needs re-levelling for it (the whole-piece loudness moved by 0.03 LU or less).

The sources lose them in three ways, none in the packer (it keeps every note of a duration):
MuseScore's export gives a short note ending where the main note begins; LilyPond's gives it a
fixed 10 or 21 ticks whatever the tempo (27 ms at 120 to the minute: a click); LilyPond 2.6's
(Mutopia's older pieces, such as Unter Donner und Blitz) keeps its written length and leaves it
sounding through the main note. A grace note is found by its shape (a quick note, or a group of up
to four, shorter than a tenth of a beat, ending where a longer note begins; not a run, not a
written trill's alternation, not a 32nd-note rhythm figure), then:

* an **acciaccatura** (a group, or before a short or distant note) is 40-70 ms, ends at the beat,
  and the note before it gives up the time (longer on the instruments that take a while to speak a
  pitch, measured with the synthesizer: violin 120 ms, clarinet 90 ms, accordion and banjo 75 ms);
  a *sung* one is 120 ms or more, begins before the beat on its own pitch, and then glides;
* an **appoggiatura** (one quick note a step from a main note of 0.45 s or more) sounds on the
  beat and takes a quarter of the main note (0.09-0.30 s); the main note ends where it was written to.

The source does not say which the score meant; this is the player's rule of thumb. A grace note
is played at 0.9 of its main note's level. The singer (src/singer.jo) sings a grace note into its
note instead of folding it into the note before: the voice needs longer than an instrument to speak
a pitch, so it starts 120 ms or more before the beat, on the grace note's own pitch (no glide into
it), holds it, then glides to the note.

Not covered: ornaments the exports do not carry at all (LilyPond's `\trill`, `\turn` and `\prall`
are held notes in the MIDI), and written-out ornaments (they are played as written).
